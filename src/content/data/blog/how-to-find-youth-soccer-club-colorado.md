Fall is here, and across Colorado — from the Front Range to the Western Slope — youth soccer tryout season is in full swing. If your player is ready to take the next step and join a club team, you're probably asking the same questions every soccer family asks: *Where do I even start? How do tryouts work? And how do I know which club is the right fit?*

This guide breaks it all down so you can walk into tryout season with confidence.

## What Is Club Soccer, and Is It Right for My Child?

Club soccer (also called travel soccer or competitive soccer) is a step up from recreational leagues. Players train multiple times a week, compete against teams from other cities and regions, and typically play in organized leagues and tournaments throughout the season.

It's a bigger commitment — in time, travel, and cost — but for players who love the game and want to develop their skills, it can be transformative.

**Club soccer might be a good fit if your child:**
- Plays recreationally and wants more challenge
- Is motivated to improve and train consistently
- Enjoys competition and team environments
- Is ready for a multi-day-per-week schedule

If your player is on the fence, attending a tryout is a low-pressure way to find out. Most clubs welcome all skill levels at tryouts, and the experience itself is valuable regardless of outcome.

## How Do Youth Soccer Tryouts Work in Colorado?

Tryouts vary by club, but the general format is consistent across most Colorado organizations.

**Typical tryout structure:**
1. **Registration** — Most clubs require you to sign up online in advance. Spots fill up, so register early.
2. **Evaluation session(s)** — Players participate in drills, small-sided games, and scrimmages. Coaches evaluate technical skill, athleticism, coachability, and soccer IQ.
3. **Notification** — Clubs typically notify families within a few days to a week after tryouts close.
4. **Commitment window** — If offered a spot, you'll have a window to accept and pay a registration deposit.

Some clubs hold multiple tryout dates; attending more than one can help if your player has a scheduling conflict or wants additional evaluation time.

## What Should I Look for in a Colorado Youth Soccer Club?

Not all clubs are the same. Here's what matters most when evaluating your options.

### Coaching Quality and Philosophy

Ask about coach credentials (USSF licenses are a good benchmark), coaching philosophy, and how they handle player development vs. winning. A club that prioritizes long-term development over short-term results tends to produce better players — and happier kids.

### Age Group and Competitive Level

Colorado clubs typically field teams at multiple age groups (U8 through U19) and competitive tiers. Make sure the club has a team at your player's age group and that the competitive level matches their current ability and goals.

### Training Schedule and Location

How many days per week does the team train? Where? Factor in commute time — a club 45 minutes away that trains three times a week is a significant commitment. Look for a club whose training location is realistic for your family's schedule.

### Club Culture and Community

Talk to other parents. Visit a practice or game before committing. A club's culture — how coaches communicate, how players treat each other, how the organization handles conflict — matters as much as the soccer.

### Cost and Financial Transparency

Club soccer costs vary widely in Colorado, from a few hundred to several thousand dollars per season when you factor in registration, uniforms, tournament fees, and travel. Ask for a full cost breakdown upfront. Many clubs offer payment plans or financial assistance.

## What Are the Main Youth Soccer Regions in Colorado?

Colorado's youth soccer landscape is organized by geography. The major regions where you'll find club tryouts include:

- **Front Range** — The most competitive region, with a high density of clubs and teams
- **Denver Metro** — Strong club presence, multiple leagues and age groups
- **Boulder / Longmont** — Active soccer community with several established clubs
- **Colorado Springs** — Growing competitive scene, especially at older age groups
- **Fort Collins / Greeley** — Northern Colorado clubs with strong community ties
- **Western Slope** — Smaller but active soccer community in Grand Junction and surrounding areas
- **Southern Colorado** — Pueblo and surrounding communities

Each region has its own clubs, leagues, and tryout schedules. The best fit for your family depends on where you live and how far you're willing to travel.

## When Do Fall/Winter Tryouts Happen?

Most Colorado clubs hold fall tryouts in October and November for teams that will compete in winter indoor leagues and spring outdoor seasons. Some clubs also hold spring tryouts for the following fall season.

Tryout windows tend to be short — often just a few weeks — so it pays to stay on top of listings and register early when you find a club that looks like a good fit.

## What Happens If My Child Doesn't Make a Team?

It happens, and it's not the end of the road. A few things to keep in mind:

- **Try multiple clubs.** Different clubs evaluate differently. A player who doesn't make one team may be a great fit at another.
- **Ask for feedback.** Many coaches are willing to share brief feedback after tryouts. Use it constructively.
- **Consider a lower tier.** Playing at a level that's slightly below your player's ceiling can build confidence and enjoyment — and sometimes leads to faster development.
- **Recreational leagues are valuable too.** There's no shame in recreational soccer. Many players thrive there and eventually move to club when the time is right.

The goal is to keep your player engaged with the game they love.
